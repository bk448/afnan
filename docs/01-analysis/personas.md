# Audience Personas

**Date:** 2026-11-24

Six divisions = six audiences. Each arrives through a different trigger event, in a
different emotional state, and converts for a different reason. The site must speak to
all six without diluting its message. Design and copy decisions trace back here.

The unifying truth: **most visitors are anxious, not shopping.** They are not comparing
five tax firms on price. They have just discovered a problem and want to know (a) how
much trouble they are in, and (b) that there is a way out. The site sells relief first,
expertise second.

---

## 1. The Catch-Up Filer / Accidental American — *the panic customer*

**Division:** `/divisions/catch-up/`
**Commercial value:** Highest. Ready to act today.

**Who they are.** A US citizen (or someone who just learned they are one) who has not
filed US returns for several years. Two sub-types:

- The *lapsed filer* — knew vaguely they should file, life got in the way, now years behind.
- The *accidental American* — born in the US but left as a child, or a dual citizen who
  never knew citizenship carried a filing obligation. Often does not feel American at all.

**Trigger event.** A foreign bank letter ("we have identified you as a US person"),
a FATCA notice, a friend's horror story, or a mortgage/account application that asked
for a US tax number. Frequently a late-night panic search.

**Emotional state.** Fear, shame, catastrophising. Believes they may owe a fortune or
face criminal exposure. This fear is usually disproportionate to reality — most owe
little and penalties are often waived through amnesty programs — and *correcting that
fear is the core job of the catch-up pages.*

**Questions they need answered.**
- Am I in legal trouble? Could I be prosecuted?
- How many years do I have to go back?
- Will the penalties bankrupt me?
- Is there an official program for people like me?
- Can I fix this quietly without triggering an audit?

**Objections.** "If I come forward, will I put myself on the IRS's radar?" "Is this firm
going to scare me into overpaying?"

**What converts them.** Calm, specific reassurance that a defined legal path exists
(Streamlined Filing), a plain explanation of what it involves, and zero pressure tactics.
A single clear next step: book a confidential consultation.

**Design implications.** Lead with reassurance, not penalties. The word "amnesty" and
the phrase "you are not alone / this is common and fixable" matter. Disclaimers must be
present but must not read as threats.

## 2. Individuals & Families Abroad — *the routine, recurring customer*

**Division:** `/divisions/individuals-families/`
**Commercial value:** Moderate per client, high in aggregate (annual retainer).

**Who they are.** An American working a normal job abroad — teacher, engineer, remote
employee, trailing spouse. Compliant or wants to be. Needs the annual return, FBAR, and
maybe FATCA done correctly and painlessly.

**Trigger event.** Tax season. Dissatisfaction with a previous preparer who did not
understand foreign income. A life change (marriage, child, house purchase).

**Emotional state.** Mild dread and annoyance rather than panic. Wants it handled so they
can forget about it.

**Questions they need answered.**
- How much will this cost? (price-sensitive)
- Do you handle both the US return and my FBAR?
- How does it work remotely — do we ever meet?
- Can you coordinate with the fact that I already pay tax here?

**Objections.** Price. "Can I just do it myself with software?" Switching cost from a
current preparer.

**What converts them.** Transparent pricing, a frictionless remote process, and evidence
the firm genuinely specialises in expats (not a generalist dabbling). The Countries pages
do heavy lifting here — a page about their specific country signals real expertise.

**Design implications.** Pricing clarity, process explanation, reassurance about remote
service. This audience actually reads `/pricing/` and `/faqs/`.

## 3. Business Owners & Entrepreneurs Abroad — *the overwhelmed high-value customer*

**Division:** `/divisions/business/`
**Commercial value:** High. Complex engagements, higher fees.

**Who they are.** An American who owns or co-owns a company abroad — a consultancy, a
startup, a freelance business that incorporated locally. Owning a foreign company
triggers some of the most punitive US reporting in the code (5471, GILTI, Subpart F).

**Trigger event.** Incorporating abroad, an accountant's warning, a GILTI surprise, or a
missed 5471 with its steep automatic penalty.

**Emotional state.** Overwhelmed and wary. Suspects their local accountant has missed
US obligations entirely. Afraid of penalties they did not know existed.

**Questions they need answered.**
- Does owning my foreign company create US filing obligations? (Yes, usually.)
- What is GILTI and does it apply to me?
- Have I already missed something, and how bad is it?
- Can you work alongside my local accountant?

**Objections.** Complexity fatigue. Cost. "My local accountant said I'm fine."

**What converts them.** Demonstrated command of entity reporting and a clear "we handle
the US side, your local accountant handles the local side" division of labour. The
Form 5471 and GILTI pages are credibility anchors.

**Design implications.** This audience respects technical depth. The Business & Entities
service pages and their linked forms should not oversimplify.

## 4. People Moving Abroad / Returning to the US — *the planner*

**Division:** `/divisions/relocation/`
**Commercial value:** Moderate. Often a one-off dual-status engagement that becomes
recurring.

**Who they are.** Someone mid-transition — relocating out of the US, or repatriating.
The year of a move is the most complex tax year a person will have (dual-status returns,
residency start/end dates, exit-state issues).

**Trigger event.** An accepted job offer abroad, an imminent move, or planning one 6–12
months out. This persona is the one most likely to arrive *before* a problem exists.

**Emotional state.** Proactive and anxious-to-get-it-right rather than panicked. Wants a
plan.

**Questions they need answered.**
- What do I need to do in the year I move?
- How do I exit my US state cleanly (California/New York residency)?
- What is a dual-status return?
- Should I do anything before I leave to save tax later?

**What converts them.** The promise of a plan and a checklist. Tax planning and the
state-tax / relocation service pages. This persona values the `/resources/deadlines/`
calendar.

**Design implications.** Forward-looking, planning-oriented copy. A consultation framed
as "get a plan before you move" converts better than "fix a problem."

## 5. Green Card Holders Abroad — *the unaware customer*

**Division:** `/divisions/green-card-holders/`
**Commercial value:** Moderate.

**Who they are.** Lawful permanent residents (green card holders) living outside the US.
Critically, they are taxed as US persons on worldwide income even while abroad — and most
do not realise this, assuming the obligation lapses when they leave.

**Trigger event.** Learning (often with alarm) that the green card carries tax obligations
regardless of physical presence; questions about abandoning the card; re-entry concerns.

**Emotional state.** Surprised, then anxious. Similar to the accidental American but with
the added dimension of immigration status consequences.

**Questions they need answered.**
- I have a green card but live abroad — do I still file US taxes? (Yes.)
- If I give up the green card, is there an exit tax?
- How do my US obligations interact with keeping my residency?

**What converts them.** Clear explanation of the overlap between tax and immigration
status, and the expatriation/exit-tax implications if they surrender the card.

**Design implications.** This persona overlaps with expatriation content. Cross-link
`/divisions/green-card-holders/` to `/services/expatriation/` and `/forms/8854/`.

## 6. High-Net-Worth, Trusts & Estates — *the discreet, advisor-led customer*

**Division:** `/divisions/private-client/`
**Commercial value:** Highest fee per engagement. Lowest volume.

**Who they are.** Wealthy individuals and families with cross-border complexity — foreign
trusts, inheritances, gifts, estates, substantial foreign assets, often working through
a wealth manager, lawyer, or family office.

**Trigger event.** A foreign inheritance or gift (Form 3520), estate planning, a trust
structure, or a referral from a lawyer / wealth advisor.

**Emotional state.** Measured, private, quality-focused. Not price-sensitive. Values
discretion and demonstrated sophistication.

**Questions they need answered.**
- Can you handle foreign trust reporting (3520 / 3520-A) and coordinate with my advisors?
- How do foreign gifts and inheritances get reported?
- What is the US gift/estate exposure on cross-border wealth?

**Objections.** Will this firm be sophisticated and discreet enough? Can they work with
my existing advisors?

**What converts them.** A restrained, authoritative presentation (the design language is
aimed squarely at this persona), evidence of trust/estate competence, and a tone that
signals discretion over marketing. This persona is why the site must not look like a
bargain SaaS funnel.

**Design implications.** This persona sets the visual ceiling. The institutional,
editorial, private-bank aesthetic exists primarily to earn *their* trust. Everything
that reassures the panic customer must also not cheapen the brand for this one.

## Design tension to resolve in Layer 2

The panic customer needs **warmth and reassurance**; the private-client customer needs
**restraint and authority**. These are not opposites if handled by *typography and tone*
rather than colour and decoration: quiet confidence reads as reassuring to the anxious
and as sophisticated to the wealthy. The design system resolves this with editorial
calm, not with two different visual modes.
